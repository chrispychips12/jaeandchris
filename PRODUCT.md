# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Wedding guests of Chris and Jae: a mixed group of Korean, Filipino and Australian family and friends across all ages, including older relatives. Most open the link on a phone from a group chat or message. They read English, Korean or Tagalog, often only one comfortably. The job: learn the date and city, feel the occasion, and leave their contact details so the formal invitation can reach them.

## Product Purpose
A one-page save the date for a wedding on Saturday 11 September 2027 in Sydney. It announces the date, counts down to it, and sends guests to a short form for their contact details. Success means every guest, whatever their language or age, gets the date, taps the button, and submits the form. A formal invitation with full details follows later.

## Brand Personality
Warm, playful, editorial. It should feel like the two of them made it: personal and a little surprising, but set with the restraint and typographic care of good printed stationery. Joyful without being loud.

## Anti-references
- Template wedding sites (Zola, The Knot, Canva-generic layouts).
- Flashy or over-animated pages where motion gets in the way of reading the date.
- Cutesy or kitschy: clip-art hearts, cartoon couples, confetti overload.
- Cold or corporate: anything that reads like a product landing page.

## Design Principles
1. The date is the message. Every beat leads to the date, the city and the button; nothing competes with them.
2. Three languages, one voice. Korean and Tagalog are first-class lines, never footnotes.
3. Stationery, then motion. It must read beautifully as a still page; motion is a reward, never a gate.
4. Personal over polished. Prefer a detail only Chris and Jae would choose over a generic wedding flourish.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA minimum, including the Korean and Tagalog lines. Respect reduced motion fully. Older guests on phones are a core audience, so tap targets and text must hold up at phone width and with enlarged system text.
